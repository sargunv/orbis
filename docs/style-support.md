# Style support

Generated by `cargo xtask support` from style-spec 26.5.1's `v8.json`,
orb-style's property table and the latest fixture results in
`docs/scratch/rt` (`native`, `native-expression`, `native-query/native`, `native-render/native`, `query/native`). Don't edit it by hand: run the fixtures, then the
task.

## Previews

New style syntax beyond the approved spec (keys, values and
expressions from unapproved proposals or our own, coordinate
reference systems) is a preview, off unless a map names it:
`MapOptions::previews` in orb-api, `previews: [...]` (or `"all"`) in
the web package, `--previews` on the CLI. With a preview off, its
inputs warn and fall back to their defaults. A preview may change or
disappear at any time. Inputs the spec's syntax already has, read
where the spec doesn't allow them, need no preview (see Properties).

Every such key, property, value and expression is named
`dev.sargunv.orbis:<name>`, with the preview of the same name
(`dev.sargunv.orbis:crs` gates the CRS projection types), whether
it is our own or from a style-spec proposal; once the spec approves a
proposal, the spec's name replaces ours. CRS identifiers (`EPSG:3413`)
and PROJ parameters keep their standard names.

## Light and atmosphere

`light.position` places the light relative to lit geometry: its
azimuth clockwise from the top of the viewport (`viewport` anchor) or
from north (`map`), its polar angle from the zenith. GL JS's and
MapLibre Native's extrusions agree on that direction. Orbis lights
everything by it: the extrusions, the ground under
`dev.sargunv.orbis:ground-lighting`, and the globe's atmosphere,
whose scattering glows over the day side, whether or not the light
shades the ground. A viewport light stands over the camera's target;
on a globe a map light stands as if over 0°, 0° for the ground and
the atmosphere, while extrusions take either anchor's light over the
target, as on a flat map. On a globe that light is one direction
across the sphere: a face far from the target is lit by how far it
turns to it there, as a wall at the limb facing it is lit.

GL JS's globe extrusions turn their normals and the light onto the
sphere by axes that keep the horizontal parts the extrusion axes
negate. That cancels at the target, but a face an arc away from it
is lit as if the light had swung twice that arc: roofs on the
light's side of the globe dim, and walls facing the light near the
limb go dark.

GL JS's globe atmosphere (`getSunPos` in `draw_sky.ts`) negates the
same vector but reads it on the view's or the globe's axes, which
turns its sun half a turn about the east axis: the north–south and
up–down parts reverse. At the spec's default light it stands behind
the globe, and the haze rims its far side, while the extrusions are
lit from the front. Native has no globe, sky or atmosphere.

## Phases

Orbis has no paint and layout: each property is evaluated in one
phase, which bounds the inputs it can follow.

| Phase | Evaluated | A change shows | What runs again | Cost | Can follow |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tile | Once per tile at the tile's zoom, on the job workers | When the tile is laid out again: an edit, or crossing a whole zoom | The layer's layout in every tile it draws | Highest: job time and uploads per tile | zoom, data |
| placement | Per camera snapshot, in placement | At the next placement | Collision and placement of the layer's labels | Placement time; no layout | zoom, camera, data |
| frame | Every frame in the shaders, per feature: property rows at the tile's zoom and the next, or the feature-state table | The next frame | Row or state-table uploads for data; node paint for the rest | Low: a uniform, or rows written once per tile | zoom, camera, data, state, elevation, daylight |
| layer | Every frame, once for the whole layer | The next frame | The node's paint | Lowest | zoom, camera, elevation |

## Properties

Inputs are `zoom`; `camera`, the camera's pitch and bearing (and with
`dev.sargunv.orbis:meters-per-pixel` its ground resolution), in values
the same for every
feature; `data`, the feature's properties, id and geometry;
`state`, its feature state; and with `dev.sargunv.orbis:daylight`
`daylight`, how lit the ground is where a label stands.
**Spec** lists the spec's expression parameters; **GL JS** and **Native**
the first versions with the property and its data-driven form (the
spec's `sdk-support`; Native is Android's); **Orbis** what Orbis reads:
the spec's inputs, then those it honours beyond the spec, marked
*accepted (not portable)*: always on, with a warning, once per
property, that GL JS and Native reject the style; then, per fixture
run (its suite and platform), the tests named for the property that
pass.
